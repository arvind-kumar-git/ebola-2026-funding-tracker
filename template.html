    Method and caveats

    • Sources. Donor press releases, Africa CDC, WHO, OCHA, UN agency appeal pages, the Congressional Research Service, and news reporting. Compiled on October 12, 2026 from search-engine extracts of those pages. The pages themselves could not be opened from the build environment, so wording may be paraphrased. Verify a figure against its link before quoting it.
    • What "counted" means. A row is counted when it is new money, specific enough to date, and not a restatement of an earlier row. Cumulative totals, allocations by pooled funds (CERF, CEPI, WHO's contingency fund) that pass donor money through, in-kind goods, and the unenacted US supplemental request are excluded. Where a donor only published a cumulative figure, a derived row reconciles the difference and is marked as such.
    • Why the headline sum is below Africa CDC's $2.997bn. Africa CDC's count includes items this tracker could not itemise, chiefly a reported EU figure above $568m and World Bank and German totals well above their published announcements. The US State Department's own count, above $2bn, sits between the two. None of the three reconcile, and the gap is itself a finding.
    • Currency. Where a source gave dollars, that figure is used. Otherwise converted at approximate mid-2026 rates: EUR 1.15, GBP 1.33, CHF 1.24, CAD 0.73, NOK 0.098, SEK 0.105, DKK 0.154. Treat converted rows as plus or minus five percent.
    • Pledge, commitment, disbursement. Donors use these words loosely. "Working with Congress, intends to provide" is recorded as a pledge. "Allocated" and "approved" are recorded as commitments. Only wording like "released", "received" or "disbursed" is recorded as disbursed.
    • Known gaps. OCHA's Financial Tracking Service and the WHO appeal funding table were not retrievable. The EU's cumulative figure, Canada's reported C$80m, a US Kenya pledge, and the Oct 2 figure of $227m mobilised for the first plan each rest on a single source. No audit of 2026 Ebola funds was found.

    Corrections and additions

    The data lives in four CSV files alongside this page: pledges.csv, appeals.csv, disbursement_evidence.csv and timeline.csv. To add or correct a row, edit the CSV, run build.py, and open a pull request at github.com/arvind-kumar-git/ebola-2026-funding-tracker. A row needs a date, an amount, a type, and a link. If you work for a donor or agency and can supply a disbursed figure, that is the single most useful contribution.
